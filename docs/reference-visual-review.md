Reviewed only the two supplied images using view_image. No implementation code, other image files, or reference identity was inspected. Provenance was not disclosed at the time of this judgment. Image resolution is excluded as a quality criterion. This is a still-image assessment; hidden geometry, motion, and actual material settings cannot be verified.

## Verdict

**B is the stronger immersive, believable image, by a clear margin.** Confidence is high for this pair, not for the underlying scenes outside these views. B combines a substantial hatch, gravity-readable fabric, varied stored objects, directional illumination, and tight spatial framing into a more convincing occupied compartment. A communicates the room layout more clearly, but too many visible forms read as repeated primitives or thin surface treatments. Neither image earns a production-quality certification from this comparison.

## Image A — five weaknesses, ranked by impact

1. **Curtain fails as suspended fabric.** Its nearly rectangular body, soft waviness, and regularly undulating bottom do not convincingly express weight, attachment tension, or fold depth. As the brightest central object, it exposes the scene's artificiality immediately.
2. **Construction and prop shapes lack credibility.** Near-duplicate rounded bags, chunky straight restraints on the case, ambiguous ceiling-run endings, and the shallow-looking hatch undermine the impression of functional equipment. More surface detail would not repair these silhouette and connection problems.
3. **Materials rely too heavily on scattered marks.** Broadly distributed chips and speckles add noise without establishing distinct paint, metal, cloth, rubber, or plastic behavior. Wear does not visibly concentrate enough around handles, exposed edges, seams, or contact points.
4. **Lighting separates into conspicuous patches.** Bright frontal cool light, opaque shadow shapes near the shelf, and saturated red wall spill do not build a persuasive continuous light environment. The red patch draws attention without revealing much useful form.
5. **Framing weakens enclosure and tension.** The straight-on hatch, generous visible floor, and regular arrangement make the room feel staged for inspection. The foreground case consumes substantial space but contributes less depth or narrative than its prominence warrants.

## Image B — five weaknesses, ranked by impact

1. **Darkness and optical effects conceal too much.** Peripheral blackening, cool haze, and bright bloom suppress shelf contents, right-side machinery, and contact boundaries. Atmosphere helps immersion but cannot substitute for readable construction; several areas cannot be judged confidently.
2. **Curtain still has weak fabric specificity.** Its large folds are persuasive, but broad smooth highlights and limited visible hem, thickness, and attachment detail leave it somewhere between cloth and a flexible sheet. The lower portion becomes especially indistinct in the glow.
3. **Camera treatment is heavy-handed.** Strong tilt, curved edge framing, and peripheral color fringing compete with the compartment itself. They increase immediacy while making proportions and room organization harder to assess. This is an optical/compositional criticism, not a resolution penalty.
4. **Secondary assemblies do not resolve clearly.** Right-side controls and cabling collapse into dark fragments; shelf supports and stored items merge. The image suggests practical complexity more effectively than it demonstrates how those parts fit together.
5. **Presentation breaks the immersive view.** The close-button overlay near the top is visibly outside the scene. The cropped dark lower-right object is also insufficiently legible to establish its role and crowds an already busy edge. Removing the overlay alone would fix only a presentation flaw, not the four scene-related weaknesses above.
